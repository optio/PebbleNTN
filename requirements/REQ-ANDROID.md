# Android Requirements

## REQ-ANDROID-001 — Platform
The application SHALL be Kotlin-based, use Compose Material 3, have minSdk 31, and use a release-time-verified Play-compatible target SDK.

**Acceptance:** Gradle configuration and CI build succeed on the documented JDK/SDK.

## REQ-ANDROID-002 — System listener
The app SHALL use `NotificationListenerService` and SHALL NOT require a permanent foreground service for normal operation.

**Acceptance:** Eligible notifications are received while the activity is closed.

## REQ-ANDROID-003 — Early package filter
The listener SHALL inspect only source metadata needed for allowlisting before deciding eligibility. It SHALL NOT read notification content for disabled packages.

**Acceptance:** Instrumented spy proves snapshot factory, parser and database are never called for a disabled package.

## REQ-ANDROID-004 — Default app enablement
Every installed app represented by the bundled navigation-app catalog SHALL be enabled on first discovery by default. The user MAY disable each app.

**Acceptance:** Fresh-install test with mocked installed packages.

## REQ-ANDROID-005 — Capture-only apps
Catalog apps without official rules MAY capture debug notifications when enabled but SHALL be labeled capture-only and SHALL NOT claim valid watch output.

## REQ-ANDROID-006 — Event processing
Processing SHALL be serialized per application process and SHALL not block the main callback thread with database or rule evaluation work.

## REQ-ANDROID-007 — No polling
The app SHALL be event-driven and SHALL NOT poll notification state, Pebble connection, or GPS continuously.

## REQ-ANDROID-008 — Debug retention
Default eligible-event retention SHALL be 1000 events with configurable limits and delete-all. The limit counts stored events; consecutive repeats of the same card are one event (REQ-DEBUG-013).

## REQ-ANDROID-009 — Settings
Settings SHALL include per-app enablement, auto-launch, retention and export privacy default. Return-to-watchface, vibration on a maneuver change and backlight on update are the phone's fixed flags (on, on and off), sent with every state message unchanged; the watch decides with its own settings whether it honours them (REQ-WATCH-015, REQ-WATCH-016). The watchapp's own settings are shown and changed on the Watch settings screen (REQ-ANDROID-021).

## REQ-ANDROID-010 — Process recovery
The app SHALL restore latest state safely and SHALL never replay obsolete turn events as a queue.

## REQ-ANDROID-011 — Master switch
The app SHALL provide a global enable/disable toggle, enabled by default. While disabled the app SHALL NOT read notification content, SHALL NOT store events, and SHALL NOT send state to the watch; the check SHALL happen before notification content is accessed. Disabling SHALL end any navigation currently shown on the watch. The setting SHALL persist across restarts.

**Acceptance:** Dispatcher unit test proving the content builder is never invoked while disabled (even for an allowlisted package); persistence test across repository instances.

## REQ-ANDROID-012 — Listener refresh
The app SHALL provide a user-initiated action that re-establishes the `NotificationListenerService` binding without a full application restart, for the case where Android has silently stopped delivering notifications to the listener while navigation is active. The action SHALL request a rebind (`NotificationListenerService.requestRebind`) and SHALL force a fresh bind by toggling the listener component's enabled state (`PackageManager` disable then enable with `DONT_KILL_APP`), ending with the component enabled; the notification-access grant SHALL be preserved across the toggle. The action SHALL be presented on the dashboard with an explanation of when to use it (navigation running on the phone but not reaching the watch), and SHALL confirm to the user that it ran. This is a recovery action only: it SHALL NOT read notification content itself and SHALL NOT change the protocol.

**Acceptance:** Unit test (Robolectric) proving the refresh leaves the listener component enabled, including recovery from a previously-disabled component. User-facing strings are Android resources; the dashboard button shows an explanation and a confirmation.

## REQ-ANDROID-013 — App update check
By default the app SHALL detect a newer version of itself with no network access, by comparing the watchapp version with the installed `versionName` (REQ-ANDROID-019). As an optional extra, it SHALL also be able to read the latest release tag from the project's GitHub Releases API and compare it against the installed `versionName`. The automatic weekly check SHALL be opt-in via a user setting that is **off by default**; when enabled it SHALL run at most once per week (on app start, only if the last check was more than a week ago). An on-demand "Check for updates" control SHALL always be available regardless of the setting, as an explicit user action. On a Google Play install the GitHub check SHALL NOT exist: no control, no weekly setting, no check on app start, and no stored GitHub data used (REQ-ANDROID-022). With the setting off and no manual check, the app SHALL make no network connection of its own. Because `INTERNET` is a normal (install-time) Android permission with no runtime prompt, the opt-in setting — not a permission request — is what gates automatic network access. The check SHALL be best-effort: any failure (offline, error, throttling) SHALL be silent and retain the last known result. This check is the app's only network access of its own, and a Google Play install makes none; the app downloads no code and no rules. The check SHALL send no user or notification data — only an unauthenticated GET of public release metadata — and SHALL NOT upload anything (REQ-SEC-002). The last-check time and last-known latest version SHALL persist. When the installed version is lower than the latest, the app SHALL show a prompt with the available and installed versions, a control to get the update from the install source (REQ-ANDROID-020), and, except on Google Play, guidance to uninstall and reinstall if installing over the top fails. The manual check SHALL confirm its outcome to the user, combined with the watchapp version (REQ-ANDROID-019). Version comparison SHALL tolerate a leading `v`, differing component counts and pre-release suffixes, and an unparseable remote tag SHALL never be treated as newer. All user-facing strings SHALL be Android resources, and the network access SHALL be reflected in the disclosure (on a Google Play install, its absence).

**Acceptance:** Unit tests for the version comparison (leading `v`, differing lengths, pre-release suffix, unparseable tag) and for the repository (weekly throttle skips within a week, forced check bypasses it, update-available detection, failure retains last known, persistence across instances, auto-check default off). Emulator verification that a manual check finds a newer release and shows the prompt with a working download link and reports its outcome, and that with auto-check off no network call is made on launch.

## REQ-ANDROID-014 — ETA carry-over
When a matched instruction carries no arrival estimate (no secondary text and no ETA epoch), the app SHALL reuse the most recent estimate observed **in the same navigation session**, so the watch's ETA does not blink out on navigation-app cards that omit it (such as Google Maps' classic turn cards). A carried estimate SHALL be reused for at most 5 minutes after it was last actually observed; reusing it SHALL NOT extend that age. It SHALL be discarded when navigation stops and SHALL never be carried into a new session. An instruction's own estimate always takes precedence and becomes the new carried estimate.

## REQ-ANDROID-015 — Temporary feedback campaign
The app MAY show a temporary dashboard card inviting the user to report a bug, request a feature or suggest a new name for the app. It SHALL appear only after the app has been used for navigation, SHALL disappear after the campaign's end date without a new release, and SHALL offer "Not now" (hidden for a while) and "Don't show again" (never shown again for that campaign), stored on the device per campaign id. Every action SHALL open the browser or the user's email app with at most the app and Android versions pre-filled; nothing SHALL be sent automatically, no notification content SHALL be included, and no new permission or network call SHALL be added. All strings SHALL be Android resources.

## REQ-ANDROID-016 — User rules that override official rules
The app SHALL show which user rules override official (bundled) rules, since user rules always take precedence and a copy keeps winning after the official rule improves. A user rule overrides an official rule when it is a copy of it (its source rule id, or the same id) or, otherwise, when it matches recent captured notifications that an official rule would handle. When a copy is made, the app SHALL record a fingerprint of the official rule, and SHALL fingerprint at startup any older copy that still equals its official rule, so that a later change to the official rule is recognised. When an official rule has changed since it was copied, the app SHALL say so on the dashboard and on the Rules screen, and offer a review listing the overlapping user rules with "Use the official rule", "Keep mine" (remembered until the official rule changes again) and "Compare". Reverting SHALL remove only user rules that overlap official rules, never other user rules, and SHALL first ask whether to share the user's rules with the developers. Wherever user rules are shown, the app SHALL explain that shared rules are reviewed and merged into the official rules in a future version, so custom behaviour is not lost. All strings SHALL be Android resources; detection SHALL run on the device only.

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the override analysis (identical, edited, newer official for unchanged and edited copies, "Keep mine" until the next change, older copies without fingerprint, same-id rules, rules taking over via captures, disabled rules); repository tests (fingerprint on copy, kept on edit, startup backfill, revert removes only the given rules); a migration test (v5 to v6); Robolectric Compose tests of the review flow (share-first dialog, revert of overlapping rules only, "Keep mine" offered only for newer official rules). Emulator verification of the dashboard card, the Rules banner and label, the review, compare and revert.

## REQ-ANDROID-017 — Tell the watch about a phone app update
While the update check (REQ-ANDROID-013) knows of a newer version, the app SHALL set flag bit 4 (`PHONE_UPDATE_AVAILABLE`, protocol 1.2) on every state message it sends to the watch — navigation update, navigation stopped and no active navigation, whose flags are otherwise 0 — and SHALL send the latest version without a leading `v` as key 14 (`LATEST_PHONE_VERSION`) on the same message. Without a known update the bit SHALL be clear and the key left out. The compatibility-error message SHALL be unchanged. The watch learns of an update from the next state message; no extra message is pushed when a check finds one (#94).

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the codec (bit 4 and key 14 without the `v` on all three state messages with the other flag bits kept; no update leaves the bit clear and the key out; the compatibility error carries neither) and of the controller reading the latest update on each send.

## REQ-ANDROID-018 — Stale update check and weekly-check question
The checks here are the optional GitHub check of REQ-ANDROID-013; the default way updates are detected is the version comparison of REQ-ANDROID-019, which needs no check to run. The update check SHALL expose when it last ran (successfully or not) and whether it is stale: never run, or last run more than 30 days ago. While stale, the dashboard SHALL show a **Check for updates** item first, above everything else including the setup checklist, and the "Check for updates now" row SHALL leave the Updates section until it is no longer stale. Tapping the item SHALL run a manual check and confirm its outcome as the manual check does. When a check started from that item finishes, whatever the outcome, and only while weekly checks are off, the dashboard SHALL ask "Check for updates automatically every week?" with **Turn on** (enables the weekly check) and **Not now** (dismisses the question). The question is not persisted, so it comes back only with the next stale item, at most once every 30 days. Reading staleness SHALL make no network call; weekly checks stay off by default (REQ-ANDROID-013). On a Google Play install, which has no GitHub check (REQ-ANDROID-022), the check is never stale, and the dashboard SHALL show neither the item, nor the question, nor the "Check for updates now" row, nor the weekly toggle, whatever an earlier install stored; GitHub, F-Droid and Obtainium installs keep this behaviour. All strings SHALL be Android resources. The onboarding disclosure describes the version comparison as the default and the GitHub check as optional (REQ-ANDROID-019); on a Google Play install it says that Google Play delivers the updates and names no GitHub check.

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the repository (never checked is stale, exactly 30 days is not, 30 days plus 1 ms is, a forced or failed check clears staleness and records its time, a throttled check does not) and of the dashboard prompt decision (stale item, question after a stale check with weekly checks off, never with weekly checks on, nothing pending, nothing on a Google Play install). A Compose test shows no update controls on a Google Play install and keeps them on a GitHub install. The dashboard layout is checked on a device.

## REQ-ANDROID-019 — Update found from the watchapp version
Both apps share one version number, and the store updates the watchapp by itself while the phone app is updated by hand, so a newer watchapp means a newer phone app has been published. When a WATCH_READY message from the watch carries the watchapp version (key 11, sent since protocol 1.0), the app SHALL store it as the last seen watchapp version, writing it only when it changes. The readiness the app makes up itself (the timed one after a launch, and the one from the watchapp being opened) carries no version and SHALL NOT change the stored one. When the stored version is newer than the installed `versionName` under the strict rule of REQ-WATCH-023 (an unparsable version is never newer), the app SHALL treat it as an available update even if the internet check never ran. The **effective update version** is the higher of the internet check's latest version, when it reports an update, and the stored watchapp version, when it is newer; it drives the update card's shown version and flag bit 4 and key 14 sent to the watch (REQ-ANDROID-017). This is the default way the app detects an update: the Core Devices Pebble app forwards WATCH_READY to the phone once the watchapp declares `companionApp` (confirmed on a phone in v0.0.74), and the watch side (REQ-WATCH-023) still covers any companion that does not. It uses no network and does not change the protocol or the internet check (REQ-ANDROID-013).

The manual check's message (REQ-ANDROID-013) SHALL combine both sources: "PebbleNTN {version} is available." whenever an effective update version exists, with any leading `v` removed, even when the internet check failed; "PebbleNTN is up to date." only when neither source reports a newer version; and "Couldn't check for updates. Try again later." when the internet check failed and the watchapp version shows no update. The choice of message SHALL be a pure function.

The dashboard Status section SHALL show a "Watchapp version" row directly below the "Watch" row: "{version} (last seen)" with the stored watchapp version shown exactly as stored, or "Not received yet" when none is stored. The row follows the stored version while the dashboard is open. Because the version has no time and is never cleared, the row proves only that a WATCH_READY arrived at least once, which lets the user check on the phone whether their companion app forwards the watch's messages. It reads only the stored version: no new storage, protocol key or network call.

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the codec (WATCH_READY with key 11 gives the version; without it, or another event, none), of the controller (a received WATCH_READY reports the version; the made-up readiness does not), of the strict version rule (the REQ-WATCH-023 cases; the tolerant GitHub comparison unchanged) and of the repository (a newer watch version is an update with no fetch; the same version again is not rewritten; an unparsable one is no update; GitHub `0.0.73` and watch `0.0.72` give `0.0.73`; GitHub `0.0.72` and watch `0.0.74` give `0.0.74`), and of the dashboard row text (`0.0.73` gives "0.0.73 (last seen)"; none or blank gives "Not received yet"; `0.0.72-debug` is shown as stored), and of the manual check's message choice (GitHub up to date with watch `0.0.75` and phone `0.0.74` gives `0.0.75`; neither newer is up to date; a failed fetch with no watchapp update fails, with one still gives the version; GitHub `v0.0.76` gives `0.0.76`). The row's place and layout are checked on a device.

## REQ-ANDROID-020 — Install source and the update card
The app SHALL detect its install source once per app start from `PackageManager.getInstallSourceInfo(packageName).installingPackageName`: **Google Play** for `com.android.vending`; **F-Droid** for `org.fdroid.fdroid`, `org.fdroid.basic`, `com.looker.droidify` and `com.machiav3lli.fdroid`; **Obtainium** for `dev.imranr.obtainium` and package names starting with `dev.imranr.obtainium.`; **GitHub** for everything else, including no installer, a browser, the package installer, adb, another store or a failed lookup. Each source has one destination: GitHub's latest release page; `https://f-droid.org/packages/com.pebblentn.app/` for F-Droid (an installed F-Droid client handles it, else the browser); for Obtainium, the Obtainium app's launch screen when it is installed, else `https://obtainium.imranr.dev/`; and for Google Play, `market://details?id=com.pebblentn.app`, else `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pebblentn.app` when no app handles it. A Google Play install gets its updates from Play only (REQ-ANDROID-022).

The update card (REQ-ANDROID-013) SHALL name the effective update version (REQ-ANDROID-019) and the installed version in text that fits any source and, except on Google Play, keeps the advice to uninstall first if the update won't install over the current app. Its main button SHALL read "Update from {source}" and open the detected source's destination. A smaller **Other sources** button SHALL list the other two of GitHub, F-Droid and Obtainium; picking one opens its destination once, and nothing is stored, so the detected source stays the default. When the detected source is F-Droid, the card SHALL add "F-Droid may take about a day to offer this update."; GitHub and Obtainium show no delay text. When the detected source is Google Play, the card SHALL read "Update from Google Play", say that a new version can take a day or two to reach Google Play, and have no **Other sources** button and no advice to uninstall first: a Play install never points to an update outside Google Play. Google Play is never listed under **Other sources**. All text SHALL be Android resources; URLs and package names are not translatable. Detection uses no network.

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the source mapping (each package name above, `com.android.vending` gives Google Play and look-alike packages do not; no installer and the package installer give GitHub) and of the card model (main source, the other two in a stable order, the delay line for F-Droid and Google Play, no other sources and no uninstall advice for Google Play). A Compose test of the Google Play card (its button opens Google Play; no other sources, no uninstall advice). The card layout, the source list and the destinations are checked on a device.

## REQ-ANDROID-021 — Watch settings screen, sync and previews
The app SHALL show and change the watchapp's own settings (REQ-WATCH-025, #102, protocol 1.3).

**Mirror and queue.** The app SHALL keep a mirror of the watch's last settings with a revision, the watch's platform and capabilities, the settings edited on the phone that the watch has not confirmed yet, and whether real settings ever arrived, and keep them across restarts. Settings arrive in a genuine WATCH_READY and in SETTINGS messages from the watch; the READY the app makes up itself carries none and SHALL NOT touch the mirror. A phone edit SHALL update the mirror, move the phone's revision on (0–99, wrapping) and be sent as SETTINGS, with only the edited settings, only while the watchapp is open: it sent its settings in this session and has not closed (the companion's app-closed callback) or failed a send since. Otherwise the edit waits, and the screen says "Will apply when the watchapp next opens"; it goes out on the next settings the watchapp sends. Settings SHALL be sent through a call that never launches or relaunches the watchapp (REQ-WATCH-006). Incoming watch settings are compared by the watch's rule: the phone keeps its edits only when it has some and its revision is newer; the same revision and values confirm them; otherwise the watch's settings win and replace the edits they carry.

**Screen.** A **Watch settings** row, first in the dashboard's Watch section (above the auto-launch switch), SHALL open a screen whose groups follow the watch menu: Appearance (colour, invert, glyph pack, arrow side), Arrival time (ETA display, ETA label), Distance (units), Backlight (duration, and colour on an RGB backlight only), Vibration (New instruction pattern and strength), Early warning (distance, and while a distance is chosen its repeat, pattern and strength), Shortcuts (Up and Down; on a touch watch Touch and, while Touch is on, the four swipes and Tap). Each row shows its value and opens a list of the values the watch offers, the current one selected: only Black and White accents on a black-and-white watch, no Next backlight colour action without an RGB backlight, the New instruction pattern with Off first. Before any settings arrived the screen says "Open PebbleNTN on your watch to load its settings"; after a watchapp older than protocol 1.3 was heard from, "Update the watchapp to change these here". Every string SHALL be an Android resource and every control SHALL have an accessibility label.

**Previews.** The list of a visual setting (colour, invert, glyph pack, arrow side, ETA display, ETA label, units) SHALL show above it the preview of the selected value, from the reported platform's image set: basalt 144×168, chalk 180×180, emery 200×228; basalt before a platform is known, and basalt in greyscale (saturation 0) for aplite and diorite. The colour list SHALL also show a swatch per row. The preview's description SHALL name the value ("Preview: Light on dark"). The images are made by `watchapp/tools/capture_screenshots.py --previews` on the emulators, one per value with every other setting at its default and the clock and instruction fixed, optimised PNGs in `drawable-nodpi` named `watch_preview_<setting>_<value>_<platform>`, and referenced statically so release builds keep them.

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the codec (a 1.2 READY still decodes and carries no settings; a 1.3 READY gives all 23 settings, the revision, the platform and the capabilities; the made-up READY gives none; SETTINGS round-trips), of the revision rule (the REQ-WATCH-025 cases), of the merge and queue (an edit while closed is sent once on the next settings, never while closed, never by a relaunching send; newer watch settings replace queued edits), of the mirror surviving a restart, of the screen (no backlight colour without RGB, no touch rows without touch, Black and White only on black-and-white, the waiting and update states, the preview naming the value) and of the preview catalogue (every visual value has an image on every set, every image maps back to a value).

## REQ-ANDROID-022 — Google Play distribution
The app SHALL be distributable on Google Play as the same app as on every other source, with no separate build: a Google Play install is detected at runtime (REQ-ANDROID-020, installer `com.android.vending`), and one signature serves every source (#114).

**Updates from Play only.** On a Google Play install the app SHALL make no network request of its own and SHALL NOT point to an update outside Google Play: the GitHub check of REQ-ANDROID-013 never runs (no control, no weekly setting, no check on app start, no stale item or weekly question, REQ-ANDROID-018), and the GitHub version, weekly setting and check time an earlier install stored SHALL be ignored, so they never show an update. The offline watchapp-version comparison (REQ-ANDROID-019) stays: a newer watchapp still shows the update card and tells the watch, and the card opens the Google Play listing (REQ-ANDROID-020). The onboarding disclosure SHALL use its Google Play variant (`spec/600-security-release/SecurityPrivacy.md`). GitHub, F-Droid and Obtainium installs SHALL behave as before.

**Target SDK.** The app SHALL target the API level Google Play requires (36 since 31 August 2026). The `verifyReleaseTargetSdk` task SHALL fail when `targetSdk` is below the floor it records, and both `release.yml` (before anything is built) and `play-release.yml` SHALL run it and fail the job when it fails.

**Upload key.** `play-release.yml` SHALL sign the App Bundle with a Play upload key kept apart from the release key, from the `PLAY_UPLOAD_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `PLAY_UPLOAD_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `PLAY_UPLOAD_KEY_ALIAS` and `PLAY_UPLOAD_KEY_PASSWORD` secrets of the protected `release` environment, and SHALL fail with a message naming the four secrets when any is empty. It attaches the bundle and its checksum to the workflow run; it uploads nothing to Play and adds no bundle to the GitHub release. No signing material is stored in the repository (REQ-SEC-007).

**Store assets and documents.** The store listing SHALL come from `fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/` (title at most 30, short description at most 80, full description at most 4,000 characters). `tools/play_store_graphics.py` SHALL produce the Play images from the committed screenshots: phone screenshots of 1080×2160, phone-size images of watch screenshots scaled up with nearest-neighbour, and a 1024×500 feature graphic; its `--check` SHALL verify the icon (512×512), the feature graphic (1024×500, no alpha) and at least two phone screenshots (each side 320–3840 px, at most 2:1, no alpha). `docs/PRIVACY_POLICY.md` and `docs/PLAY_DATA_SAFETY.md` SHALL state every network use and outbound link of the app, and claim nothing it does not do; `docs/PLAY_CONSOLE.md` SHALL hold every Play Console answer and step (App content, notification access, store listing, signing, release procedure, closed test and developer verification).

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the source mapping (`com.android.vending` gives Google Play), of the update policy per source, of the repository on a Google Play install (no fetch, a stored GitHub version and weekly setting ignored, a newer watchapp still an update), of the update card and prompt decision for Google Play, and Compose tests of the dashboard (no update controls on Google Play, unchanged on GitHub) and of the onboarding disclosure variant. `verifyReleaseTargetSdk` passes at the floor and fails below it (checked by a temporary local change). `tools/play_store_graphics.py --check` and its unit tests run in `scripts/test-all.sh` when Pillow is available. On an Android 16 phone: onboarding, the dashboard with 3-button and gesture navigation, the rule editor's back and discard dialog, and a navigation session to the watch.

## REQ-ANDROID-023 — App variants in the catalog
A catalog app MAY use another app's rules (`rulesFrom`, REQ-RULE-015), MAY name an https `website` where it is obtained, and MAY be hidden on Google Play (`playListed: false`, default true). MicroG Maps (`microg-maps`, package `app.morphe.android.apps.maps`, Google Maps patched for microG) is such an app: it uses the Google Maps rules, its website is `https://github.com/fangkampanat/gmaps-patches`, and it is not listed on Google Play (#113). Its package SHALL be in the manifest `<queries>` like every catalog package.

On a GitHub, F-Droid or Obtainium install, an installed variant SHALL be its own Navigation apps row with its own name, icon and switch (REQ-ANDROID-004, REQ-ANDROID-009), showing and opening the rules it uses; when not installed, its link SHALL open its `website` instead of Google Play, and the debug history SHALL name it. Every other app keeps its Google Play link.

On a Google Play install (REQ-ANDROID-022), an app with `playListed: false` SHALL NOT be listed anywhere and its website SHALL NOT be linked or named. If it is installed, its notifications SHALL still be processed with the rules it uses, gated by the switch of the app named by `rulesFrom` (its package joins that app's row; that app's row is created when only the variant is installed). The shared store description does not name it.

**Acceptance:** Unit tests of the catalog parser (the new fields, the defaults, an http website rejected), of the Google Play view of the catalog (no MicroG Maps entry, its package in Google Maps' entry after Google Maps' own, no website), of enablement (own row and switch on GitHub; on Google Play the Google Maps switch on lets a MicroG Maps notification through to the rules and off stops it), Compose tests of Navigation apps (GitHub: the MicroG Maps row, its website link and accessibility label, Google Play links for the others; Google Play: no MicroG Maps row or link), and `scripts/validate_catalog.py` and its unit tests (26 navigation packages in `<queries>`, a missing one reported).

# Watch Requirements

## REQ-WATCH-001 — Renderer
The watchapp SHALL render normalized instructions and SHALL NOT parse Android notification text.

## REQ-WATCH-002 — Bitmap maneuvers
Maneuver graphics SHALL use bundled bitmap resources.

## REQ-WATCH-003 — READY
The watchapp SHALL send READY after AppMessage initialization.

## REQ-WATCH-004 — Current state
After READY, the phone SHALL send only the latest current state.

## REQ-WATCH-005 — Automatic launch
The phone SHALL automatically launch the watchapp once when a new navigation session starts by default. This SHALL be user-configurable.

## REQ-WATCH-006 — No focus stealing loop
The phone SHALL NOT relaunch the watchapp merely to bring it to the foreground during the same session after the user leaves it, and SHALL NOT relaunch it on a timer or poll. It MAY re-launch the watchapp when a companion app refuses to deliver a pending state update unless the watchapp is the foreground app on the watch — notably the Core Devices Pebble app, which returns `FailedDifferentAppOpen` in that case — because otherwise the update cannot reach the watch at all. Such a re-launch SHALL be driven only by an actual pending state update (never speculatively), so it occurs at most as often as the navigation state materially changes. See `spec/200-architecture/Pebble.md` → Launch policy.

## REQ-WATCH-007 — Navigation stop
On navigation stop, the watch SHALL return to the watchface by default. This SHALL be user-configurable.

## REQ-WATCH-008 — Low power
The watchapp SHALL avoid polling, continuous animation and unnecessary redraws.

## REQ-WATCH-009 — Vibration
The watch SHALL not vibrate on every distance update; maneuver-change vibration is configurable.

See REQ-WATCH-016 for the on-watch vibration setting (pattern and strength) layered on top of this, and REQ-WATCH-022 for the user-opted early warning, the one case where a distance update alone may vibrate.

## REQ-WATCH-010 — Compatibility
Protocol incompatibility SHALL produce an explicit state rather than silent failure.

## REQ-WATCH-011 — Appearance settings
The watchapp SHALL provide on-watch settings for accent colour, inverted display, distance units, glyph pack and arrow corner, applied immediately and persisted across launches. The arrow-corner setting SHALL choose which top corner holds the maneuver arrow (top-right by default), placing the distance in the opposite corner. The colour list SHALL be reduced to the colours the watch can display: 16 accents on colour watches (e.g. Pebble Time 2), black/white on black-and-white watches (e.g. Pebble 2 Duo), so both polarities and their inverses remain reachable on every model. Accent colour and glyph pack, each being a list rather than a toggle, SHALL each open their own sub-menu rather than sharing the top settings screen. On colour watches the focused row of the accent list SHALL be drawn in that accent, with legible text, so scrolling previews each colour; the white accent's focused row SHALL be framed so the focus stays visible (#81).

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification on a colour platform (basalt), a black-and-white platform (diorite) and the large-screen platform (emery / Pebble Time 2).

## REQ-WATCH-013 — Screen-size adaptation
The watchapp SHALL detect the watch's screen size at runtime and adapt its layout without a user setting: the 144-wide models (aplite/basalt/diorite) and the round chalk display use the compact layout, while Pebble Time 2 (emery, 200×228) uses a larger layout — bigger status-strip fonts, a larger maneuver arrow (bundled as an emery-only resource so the smaller models never carry it) and a maneuver panel sized to hug the arrow rather than a fixed fraction of the screen. Adaptation SHALL keep every supported model rendering correctly.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that a 144-wide platform and emery each render the compact and large layouts respectively.

## REQ-WATCH-012 — Glyph packs
The watchapp SHALL provide built-in maneuver glyph packs selectable on the watch, defaulting to the Classic pack. The glyph-pack menu SHALL let the user preview each pack on the watch before selecting it. Packs are a render-only choice: the phone SHALL continue to send only the maneuver code, and pack selection SHALL NOT change the protocol. An unclassified maneuver (UNKNOWN) SHALL render a question-mark fallback glyph.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that switching packs re-renders the current maneuver, that the preview shows each pack, and that UNKNOWN renders the "?" glyph.

## REQ-WATCH-014 — ETA display mode
The watchapp SHALL provide an on-watch setting selecting how the status strip renders the arrival estimate, applied immediately and persisted across launches. It SHALL offer two modes and default to the arrival-time mode: **arrival time** displays the phone-provided arrival clock time, and **time to arrival** displays the remaining duration until arrival in `H:MM` form, computed on the watch and recomputed at least once per minute. This is a render-only choice: mode selection SHALL NOT change the protocol; the phone SHALL continue to send the arrival-time string, and the arrival epoch (`etaEpochSeconds`) whenever a rule extracts one.

The countdown SHALL be derived in this order:
1. from the arrival epoch when the phone sent one, to the exact second (rounded up to the next whole minute);
2. otherwise from the arrival-time string, when that string contains a wall-clock time, by comparing it against the watch's own clock — both being wall-clock time in the phone's timezone. The comparison SHALL wrap over midnight, so an arrival past midnight reads as minutes away rather than a negative or a whole day. Only a genuine clock time SHALL be accepted: a value shaped like a time but out of range (`25:00`) SHALL NOT be reinterpreted as some other time.

The string is a normalized field the phone chose to send, not notification text, so deriving from it does not weaken REQ-WATCH-001. When neither source yields a time — the mode is time-to-arrival, no epoch was sent, and the arrival-time string is absent or is not a clock time — the watch SHALL fall back to displaying the arrival-time string with the arrival-time label rather than leaving the strip blank.

A second on-watch setting, **ETA label** (#13), SHALL choose how the small label before the readout is written, applied immediately and persisted across launches. The menu SHALL show each style as the labels it produces. **"ETA at / ETA in"** (the default) labels the arrival time "ETA at" and the countdown "ETA in"; **"ETA / IN"** labels them "ETA" and "IN". When the "ETA at" / "ETA in" label does not fit between the clock and the readout, the watch SHALL stack it on two lines ("ETA" over "at" / "in"); only when even that does not fit SHALL it use "ETA" / "IN" for that redraw.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that the setting toggles the strip between "ETA at 14:35" and an "ETA in H:MM" countdown, that the ETA label setting switches both to "ETA" / "IN", that the label stacks on two lines on a 144-wide screen when it does not fit on one, that the countdown updates as the clock advances, that it is derived from the arrival-time string when no epoch is sent (including 12-hour strings such as "2:35 PM" and arrivals across midnight), and that a non-time arrival string falls back to the arrival-time display. Host unit tests cover the string parsing, the midnight wrap and the label texts.

## REQ-WATCH-015 — Backlight setting
The watchapp SHALL provide an on-watch setting that keeps the backlight lit while the watchapp is the foreground app, applied immediately and persisted across launches. It SHALL default to **Watch default** — no additional backlight, leaving the watch's own automatic light behaviour untouched — and offer three further intensity levels. Because the Pebble SDK exposes no backlight *brightness* control to apps (only on/off via `light_enable`), the three levels SHALL express intensity as how long the light is held engaged around navigation activity: **Low** and **Medium** light the backlight for a short and a longer period respectively after each navigation update, and **High** keeps it steady-on for the whole session. The setting SHALL override the watch's automatic control only while a non-default level is selected, and SHALL release control back to the watch (`light_enable(false)`) when switched back to Watch default or when the app exits. While a non-default level is selected it supersedes the phone's per-update backlight request (REQ-WATCH is otherwise unchanged when the setting is at its default). This is a watch-side render/behaviour choice: it SHALL NOT change the protocol.

Each level's menu label SHALL state what the level does rather than only how strong it is, so the choice is self-explanatory on the watch: "Watch default", "Low - 3s on update", "Medium - 10s on update", "High - until app closes".

**Backlight colour (RGB hardware only).** On watches whose backlight LED is colour-capable — identified at compile time by `PBL_RGB_BACKLIGHT`, currently Pebble Time 2 — the watchapp SHALL additionally offer a backlight **colour**, applied immediately and persisted across launches. It SHALL default to **Watch default** (the user's own backlight colour, restored with `light_set_system_color()`) and offer a list of tints applied with `light_set_color_rgb888()`, chosen over `light_set_color()` because `GColor` carries only 2 bits per channel. The tint SHALL be pushed to the LED whenever the watchapp engages the light and whenever the colour setting changes. No teardown is required: the system resets the tint when the app exits or a notification preempts it.

On hardware without an RGB backlight the colour setting SHALL NOT be offered at all, and the Backlight row SHALL keep cycling the duration in place. Where the colour setting exists, the Backlight row SHALL instead open a sub-window holding both the **Duration** and the **Colour**, the latter opening a colour list of its own.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that Watch default leaves the light unforced, that High keeps the backlight on for the whole session, that Low/Medium light it around updates and release it after their hold, and that switching back to Watch default releases the light. For the colour setting: emulator verification on an RGB platform (emery) that the Backlight row opens a Duration + Colour sub-window, that the colour list marks and applies the selection, and that both survive an app relaunch; and on a non-RGB platform (basalt) that the Backlight row still cycles the duration in place and offers no colour. The rendered LED tint itself is observable only on hardware.

## REQ-WATCH-016 — Vibration setting
The watchapp SHALL provide on-watch settings that vibrate the watch when a new navigation instruction is parsed, applied immediately and persisted across launches: a **pattern** row and a **strength** row. The pattern row SHALL default to **Off** (no watch-initiated vibration) and offer Single, Double, Triple and Long patterns, differing in the number/shape of pulses. The strength row SHALL offer Light, Medium (default) and Strong; because the Pebble SDK exposes no vibration *amplitude* control, strength SHALL be expressed as pulse length (a longer buzz reads as stronger), realised with a custom vibration pattern. Vibration SHALL fire only on genuinely new information — a changed maneuver or changed primary text — never on a plain resend of the same instruction as the distance counts down (REQ-WATCH-009). When the pattern is a non-Off value it supersedes the phone's simple maneuver-change pulse; when it is Off, the original maneuver-change vibration (gated by the phone's request) stands. Changing either row in the menu SHALL play the newly selected buzz as a preview. This is a watch-side behaviour choice: it SHALL NOT change the protocol. The two rows live in the **New instruction** section of the Vibration sub-window (REQ-WATCH-022).

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that Off preserves the original maneuver-change pulse, that each pattern plays its number of pulses on a new instruction, that Light/Medium/Strong produce progressively longer buzzes, that a resend of the same instruction does not vibrate, and that changing a row previews the buzz.

## REQ-WATCH-017 — Companion-app connection hint
On launch the watchapp SHALL send WATCH_READY (REQ-WATCH-003) and wait to hear back from the phone. Because the SDK gives the watchapp no way to query whether the PebbleNTN Android companion app is installed — `connection_service_peek_pebble_app_connection()` reports only whether the *Pebble mobile app* is connected, and on Android PebbleKit connectivity merely mirrors that — the only reliable signal that the companion app is present and functioning is receiving any AppMessage in reply. The companion app always answers WATCH_READY with the current state (a navigation update, or NO_ACTIVE_NAVIGATION when idle), so a reply confirms it is installed and working.

While waiting, the connection state ("Connecting") SHALL show a countdown of the seconds remaining, since a healthy handshake completes within a second or two. If no message arrives within a 5-second timeout, the watchapp SHALL surface a hint whose content depends on the Pebble mobile app connection:
- **Pebble app connected** (the companion app is the likely missing piece): show an install hint with a QR code that encodes the GitHub releases page, where the companion APK is published. The QR SHALL be drawn on a white field regardless of the appearance theme, so its quiet zone stays scannable.
- **Pebble app not connected**: show "Open the Pebble app" instead, since installing the companion app cannot help until the phone link is up.

The watchdog SHALL be retired permanently once any message is received, so a real state can never be overwritten by the hint, and SHALL restart when the Pebble app reconnects, so a watch that was out of range gets a fresh chance to hear back. This is a watch-side behaviour choice: it SHALL NOT change the protocol. The QR encodes a fixed URL and is a committed static resource, not generated at build time.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that the countdown is shown and counts down; that a navigation update arriving during the countdown cancels the watchdog and no hint appears afterwards; that with the Pebble app connected but no reply the install QR is shown after the timeout; that with the Pebble app disconnected the "Open the Pebble app" text is shown; and that a message arriving after the hint replaces it with the real state. The QR's on-device scannability is a hardware check.

## REQ-WATCH-018 — Public-transit legs
The watchapp SHALL render the TRANSIT maneuver (protocol maneuver code 12) with a dedicated transit (train) glyph in every glyph pack and screen size (REQ-WATCH-012, REQ-WATCH-013). When a TRANSIT state carries `stopsRemaining` (protocol key 13, added in protocol 1.1), the watch SHALL show the stop count in the distance area in place of a distance, as the number with a "stop"/"stops" unit; without it, the distance area follows the usual distance rules. A watchapp that predates protocol 1.1 SHALL still degrade gracefully: it ignores the unknown key and renders the unknown-maneuver "?" glyph for code 12.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification that a TRANSIT state renders the transit glyph with "N stops" in each glyph pack, on a 144-wide platform and on emery; host unit test of the stop-count formatting.

## REQ-WATCH-019 — Launcher subtitle (app glance)
On platforms that support app glances (all except aplite), the watchapp SHALL set its launcher subtitle when it closes. While a route is active and the phone sent an arrival time that is still ahead, the subtitle SHALL be a live countdown with the arrival-time text, "In 0:24 · ETA 13:24" (countdown only when no arrival-time text was sent), which the launcher keeps current and which SHALL expire at the arrival time. Otherwise, after it expires, and once navigation has stopped or no navigation is active, the subtitle SHALL be "Update: {version}" while a phone app update is recorded (REQ-WATCH-023, #94), else the hint "Navigate on phone". The characters the template language treats specially (`{`, `}`, `\`) SHALL be dropped from the arrival-time text and from the version. Aplite SHALL be unaffected; it relies on the update notice and idle text.

**Acceptance:** Host unit test of the subtitle text (countdown with and without arrival-time text, no route, no or past arrival time, template characters, buffer size; the update text, its template characters, empty version and buffer size). Emulator verification on a 144-wide platform and on emery that the countdown appears and counts down after leaving the app during a route, that it falls back to the hint (or, with an update recorded, the update text) at the arrival time, and that the hint shows after navigation stops; and on basalt, chalk and emery that the update text shows in the launcher.

## REQ-WATCH-020 — About screen
The settings menu SHALL end with an **About** row, showing the watchapp version as its subtitle, that opens a read-only screen with, in this order: the watchapp version ("Watchapp 0.0.68"); the last connected phone app version ("Phone app 0.0.67 (last seen)", or "Phone app: never connected" when none was ever recorded) (#103); the protocol version; the phone connection as the watch sees it ("Phone app connected" once the PebbleNTN phone app has answered since the watchapp opened, "Phone app not answering" when only the Pebble app is connected, "Pebble app not connected" otherwise); and, under "Install phone app" (#14), the same install QR as the connection hint (REQ-WATCH-017) with its link as text below it. The screen SHALL be black on white whatever the theme, so the QR's quiet zone stays white, SHALL scroll with UP/DOWN where it is taller than the screen, and SHALL keep its text clear of the bezel on a round display. It adds no image resources.

The last connected phone app version is the `APP_VERSION` the phone already sends with its messages, kept to its first 16 bytes; an empty value counts as none. The watchapp SHALL persist it, writing only when it changes, so it is still shown after an app relaunch and while the phone is out of range. About SHALL take it, like the connection status, when it opens, and SHALL NOT refresh while open. Recording it needs no phone app change.

**Acceptance:** Host unit tests of the About text (phone-status cases, the details lines with and without a phone version, a worst case of 16-byte versions with the longest status line that fits uncut, truncation) and of the version-recording rule (first version recorded, same version not rewritten, new version recorded, empty or missing version ignored, a longer version cut to 16 bytes). Emulator verification on basalt, chalk, emery and diorite that the row and the screen render, that a fresh install shows "Phone app: never connected", that a phone message with a version shows it as "last seen" and that it is still shown after an app relaunch without the phone, that the screen scrolls to the link, and that it stays within the bezel on chalk.

## REQ-WATCH-021 — Touch in the settings menus
On a touch watch (Pebble Time 2), the watchapp SHALL opt into the system touch navigation bridge, so the settings menu, its sub-lists and the About screen scroll, highlight, select and go back by touch, like the watch's own menus. They SHALL respond to touch **only while touch navigation is on in the watch's system settings**: the watchapp has no touch switch of its own for the menus and handles no touch events itself in those windows. The navigation screen SHALL opt out of the bridge, so a stray swipe can't close the app mid-route; its configurable gestures are REQ-WATCH-024.

**Acceptance:** Emulator verification on emery (touches sent over VNC) that the settings menu scrolls, highlights and activates a row by tap, goes back by a right swipe, that About scrolls, and that the navigation screen stays open after a right swipe. Hardware check on a Pebble Time 2 that the menus respond with system touch navigation on and ignore touch with it off.

## REQ-WATCH-022 — Early-warning vibration
The watchapp SHALL offer an **early warning**: a vibration as the next maneuver comes within a user-chosen distance, with its own pattern and strength so it can be told apart from the new-instruction buzz (REQ-WATCH-016). Its settings SHALL be applied immediately and persisted across launches:
- **Distance**: Off (default), 10, 25, 50, 100 or 200 m. The threshold is in metres; with imperial units the label SHALL show the nearest round feet value (30, 80, 160, 330, 650 ft).
- **Repeat**: **Once** (default) buzzes the first time an update is at or under the threshold for an instruction, and re-arms only on new information (a changed maneuver or primary text). **Every update** buzzes on each update at or under the threshold.
- **Pattern** (Single, Double — the default —, Triple, Long; no Off, which is what an Off distance is for) and **Strength** (Light, Medium — the default —, Strong), realised as in REQ-WATCH-016.

A plain resend of the distance already warned about SHALL NOT buzz again. When one update is both new information and at or under the threshold, the new-instruction buzz (watch pattern, or the phone's maneuver-change pulse) SHALL take precedence and count as the warning, so the two never stack; when no new-instruction buzz plays, the warning SHALL play. The warning SHALL NOT fire for a transit ride (REQ-WATCH-018) or when the distance is unknown, and its state SHALL reset when navigation stops or no navigation is active.

The settings menu SHALL hold all vibration settings behind a single **Vibration** row whose subtitle summarises both ("Double / Warn 50 m", "Off / Warn off"). It opens a sub-window with a **New instruction** section (Pattern, Strength — REQ-WATCH-016) and an **Early warning** section (Distance, and, only while a distance is chosen, Repeat, Pattern and Strength). Changing a pattern, strength or distance row SHALL play the selected buzz as a preview. Settings stored by earlier versions SHALL carry over unchanged. This is a watch-side behaviour choice: it SHALL NOT change the protocol.

**Acceptance:** Host unit test of the decision (Once fires once per instruction at the crossing, Every fires per new distance, resends are quiet, precedence of the new-instruction buzz, transit and unknown distance are quiet) and of the threshold labels in both units. Emulator verification on basalt, chalk and emery that the Vibration row and sub-window render with their defaults, that the early-warning rows appear once a distance is chosen, that the buzz fires per the above for a scripted route, and that the settings survive an app relaunch. The motor itself is a hardware check.

## REQ-WATCH-023 — Phone app update notice
Since protocol 1.2 the phone sets flag bit 4 (`PHONE_UPDATE_AVAILABLE`) on every state message while it knows of a newer phone app, with the latest version as key 14 (`LATEST_PHONE_VERSION`, REQ-ANDROID-017). On each inbound message that carries `FLAGS`, the watchapp SHALL record that version (kept to its first 16 bytes) while the bit is set and the key is a non-empty string, SHALL keep the recorded version when the bit is set without one, and SHALL clear it when the bit is clear. It SHALL persist the recorded version and the version the notice was last shown for, each written only when it changes, and load both at startup (#94).

While a version is recorded that the notice has not yet been shown for, the watchapp SHALL show a full-screen notice reading "A phone app update is available. Open the mobile app to find the update.", black on white whatever the theme and clear of the bezel on a round display, with no vibration and no backlight change. It SHALL close after 10 seconds or at once on any button, and a press SHALL only close it, never act on the screen behind. It SHALL be marked shown for that version as it opens, and SHALL appear again only for a different version. It SHALL NOT open during a route or over Settings or About: it waits for the next "no navigation" message, or for a navigation stop that leaves the app open. When the app exits to the watchface on stop, it stays unshown until a later start with no route active.

While a version is recorded, the "No navigation detected" screen SHALL read "No navigation detected. Phone app update available: open the mobile app." in place of "No navigation detected. If navigating, open PebbleNTN on your phone.", every time it shows. The launcher subtitle follows REQ-WATCH-019. A watchapp that predates protocol 1.2 ignores the bit and the key.

The watch SHALL also detect an update with no internet check, by comparing versions. Both apps share one version number and the store updates the watchapp by itself, so a watchapp newer than the phone app means a newer phone app has been published. On each inbound message that carries the phone app version (key 11), and at startup from the stored phone version (REQ-WATCH-020), the watchapp SHALL compare its own version with it under the strict rule: a version is parsable when, after an optional leading `v` or `V`, it is one or more `.`-separated parts of 1 to 9 decimal digits and nothing else; two parsable versions compare numerically part by part from the left, missing parts counting as 0 (`0.0.9` < `0.0.10`, `1.2` = `1.2.0`); a comparison involving an unparsable or missing version is never "newer". The **effective update version** is the higher of the recorded version (above) and the watchapp's own version when it is newer than the phone app; when neither applies there is no update, and a message with bit 4 clear SHALL NOT clear the watch's own finding. The notice (once per version), the update idle text and the launcher subtitle SHALL all use the effective update version, with their text and timing unchanged. No persist key SHALL be added: the effective version is derived from the stored phone version, the stored recorded version and the watchapp's own version, and the shown-version record works as before.

**Acceptance:** Host unit tests of the recording rule (first version, same again, flag clear clears, missing or empty version keeps, 16-byte cap), of the show/defer/skip decision (new version, once shown, newer version, during a route, another screen on top, no update), of the idle text, and of the strict version rule and the effective update version (`0.0.10` > `0.0.9`, `1.2` = `1.2.0`, a leading `v`, `0.0.71-debug`, `0.0.x`, `0..1`, empty, missing and 10-digit parts rejected; own newer, reported higher, own not newer, phone version unparsable). Emulator verification on basalt, chalk, emery and diorite that the notice appears, closes after 10 s and on a button without acting behind it, does not reappear after a relaunch, appears again for a newer version, waits during a route and appears on stop, and that both idle texts stay within the bezel; and, with key 11 below the watchapp's version and bit 4 clear, that the notice appears once, the idle text shows the update and the subtitle reads `Update: {watchapp version}`, while key 11 equal to the watchapp's version shows no update.

## REQ-WATCH-024 — Shortcuts: buttons and touch gestures on the navigation screen
The settings menu SHALL have a **Shortcuts** row on every watch, just above About (About stays last), whose subtitle summarises the button shortcuts ("Up: None / Down: None"). It opens a sub-window listing **Up button** and **Down button** on every watch and, on a touch watch (Pebble Time 2) while `touch_service_is_enabled()` is true, a **Touch** row (On / Off, toggled in place) followed, while Touch is On, by **Swipe left**, **Swipe right**, **Swipe up**, **Swipe down** and **Tap** (#12). Each shortcut row shows its action as its subtitle and opens an action list marking the current action "Selected"; picking one saves it and returns. Every choice SHALL apply at once and persist across launches, under new persist keys only (19 Touch, 20–26 one action per slot, 27 the last New instruction pattern that wasn't Off); keys 1–18 are unchanged.

**Defaults:** Touch Off; Up, Down, Swipe up, Swipe down and Tap None; Swipe left and Swipe right "ETA: arrival time / time left". A watch with none of these stored SHALL behave as before: UP and DOWN do nothing and no touch reacts.

**Actions**, each through the same setter and save as the settings menu, with the notice it shows:
- None: nothing, no notice.
- ETA: arrival time / time left: toggles the ETA display mode (REQ-WATCH-014); "Arrival time" / "Time left".
- ETA label style: toggles "ETA at / ETA in" and "ETA / IN"; the new style's name.
- Next colour / Previous colour: the next or previous accent, wrapping; black-and-white watches cycle Black and White only; the colour's name.
- Invert colours: "Light on dark" / "Dark on light".
- Swap arrow side: "Arrow left" / "Arrow right".
- Next glyph pack: Classic, Bold, Outline, wrapping; "Classic glyphs" / "Bold glyphs" / "Outline glyphs".
- Vibration on / off: the New instruction pattern (REQ-WATCH-016) only; a pattern other than Off becomes Off, Off becomes the last pattern that wasn't Off (Single if none), which then plays as a preview. The early warning and the phone's pulse are untouched. "Vibration off" / "Vibration: {pattern}".
- Early warning: next distance / previous distance: steps Off, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200 m, wrapping (REQ-WATCH-022); "Early warning off" / "Early warning {distance}", the distance in the chosen units.
- Backlight now: lights the backlight now (`light_enable_interaction`); no notice.
- Next backlight colour: the next backlight colour, wrapping; offered only on RGB-backlight watches (REQ-WATCH-015); the colour's name.
- Units: metric and imperial; the units' name.
- Open settings: the same as SELECT; no notice.

The navigation screen SHALL redraw at once, as on returning from Settings. A notice SHALL show as a small plain-text banner over the road area for about 1.5 seconds, in the road area's colours swapped; a second action within that time SHALL replace its text and restart the timer.

**Input.** UP and DOWN single presses on the navigation window run their shortcuts; SELECT still opens Settings and BACK still closes the app. On a touch watch, swipes and taps SHALL be recognised by the firmware's swipe and tap recognizers attached to the navigation window, which keeps the system touch bridge off (REQ-WATCH-021), so no touch can close the app. Gestures SHALL run only while Touch is On and `touch_service_is_enabled()` is true. Touch code is compiled only for emery; the button shortcuts exist on every platform, aplite included, within its heap. This is a watch-side choice: it SHALL NOT change the protocol or the phone app.

**Acceptance:** Host unit test of the pure shortcuts unit (defaults, sanitising a stored value, wrap-around cycling at both ends, the vibration toggle with and without a stored pattern, the action list with and without an RGB backlight, every notice text, truncation in a small buffer, a label for every action). Emulator verification that basalt and aplite show Shortcuts with exactly the two button rows; that on basalt UP with Up = Invert colours inverts the road area, shows the banner, which is gone 2 s later, and that a second press within 1.5 s replaces it; that on emery a fresh install shows Touch Off with no gesture rows, that the five rows appear once Touch is On, that with Touch On a tap runs the Tap action with its notice, and that with Touch Off taps and swipes change nothing. Swipes on the emery emulator reach the watch as taps (VNC sends no touch movement), so the swipe directions, and a swipe not also running the Tap action, are checked on a Pebble Time 2.
